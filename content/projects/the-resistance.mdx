## The evidence gap

AI agents increasingly interact with tools, enterprise applications, APIs, and sensitive data. A useful security investigation needs to understand what an agent accessed, which actions it attempted, what authorization applied, and whether activity crossed expected boundaries.

Traditional logs may capture individual requests without the context of the whole agentic workflow. The Resistance explores how those fragments could become evidence a security team can actually use.

## Two engineering streams

### The data pipeline

The current development direction centers on Java-based event processing, Kafka integration, event validation, and reliable consumption. Normalization into OCSF-aligned security events, enrichment, and correlation are planned directions. Specific implementation milestones have not been independently verified on this website.

### The AI Investigator

Python-based services are being developed around evidence retrieval, event-reference validation, and investigation findings. The longer-term direction includes agentic investigation orchestration. Findings should remain connected to the evidence that supports them, with human review where judgment is required.

## A shared model, not a black box

The conceptual architecture above describes a destination, not a claim that every stage is implemented. Collection should preserve source context. Processing should validate and normalize records. Investigations should cite identifiable evidence. Findings should make uncertainty clear.

Potential remediation workflows are a future exploration. No autonomous remediation or production deployment is claimed.
